title: Writing a vehicle description people finish reading
topic: listing
summary: Nobody reads a wall of capital letters. Four short paragraphs, written like a person, outsell a spec dump every time.
---

Most used car descriptions are either blank or a wall of shouting: **CLEAN CARFAX!!! MUST SEE!!! WON'T LAST!!!** Both lose you money. The blank one gives a buyer nothing to act on. The shouting one sounds like every scam they have been warned about.

## What the customer is actually asking

Behind every listing view are four questions:

1. Is this car going to break on me?
2. Why is it this price?
3. What is wrong with it?
4. Who am I dealing with?

A good description answers all four in under 150 words. That is it. That is the whole job.

## The shape that works

**Open with what it is and who it suits.**

> 2019 Hyundai Tucson SE, front-wheel drive, just over 104,000 miles. Good size for a small family — real back seat, hatch big enough for a stroller and a grocery run.

**Say what you know about its condition.**

> We put it through our inspection when it came in: brakes and fluids are good, tires have plenty of life. Runs and drives clean, no warning lights.

**Say the flaw.**

> There's a scuff on the rear bumper, driver's side — you can see it in the photos. We priced it accordingly rather than fixing it and charging for it.

**Close with the next step, not a slogan.**

> It's on the lot in Middletown. Call and we'll have it up front and ready to drive when you get here.

That is four short paragraphs and it does more than 400 words of features.

## Rules that keep it readable

- **Sentence case.** ALL CAPS reads as shouting and is genuinely harder to read.
- **No more than three exclamation points per lot.** Per lot, not per listing.
- **Cut anything that could describe any car.** "Great condition," "runs great," "well maintained" are noise. If it is true, prove it with a specific.
- **Numbers beat adjectives.** "New front pads in March" beats "excellent brakes."
- **Never write "must see."** Everyone writes it. It means nothing.

## Say the flaw on purpose

This is the part people fight. The instinct is to hide the scratch and hope they do not notice.

They notice. They notice at the walkaround, after they drove forty minutes, and now the entire conversation is about whether you were hiding anything else. You lose the sale and you earn a review about it.

Naming the flaw yourself does three things: it filters out people who would have walked anyway, it makes your price make sense, and it makes every other claim in the listing credible. It is the cheapest trust you will ever buy.

## Things you cannot write

Do not promise financing outcomes. "All credit types considered, subject to lender approval" is accurate. "Guaranteed approval" and "everyone rides" are claims we cannot back, and they are exactly the language regulators look for.

Do not invent a rate or a payment in the description. Point them at the estimator and let them run their own numbers.

## The test

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a person describing a car to a neighbor, ship it. If it sounds like a radio ad, delete it and write the neighbor version.
