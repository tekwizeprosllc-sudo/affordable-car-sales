---
title: Follow-up texts that get a reply the same day
summary: Most follow-up fails because it asks for nothing and offers nothing. Short, specific, and easy to answer wins.
---

The average follow-up text is "Hi, just checking in to see if you're still interested in the vehicle!" It gets ignored, and it deserves to be. It asks the customer to do all the work of restarting the conversation.

## Three rules

**Be specific about the car.** "The Equinox" beats "the vehicle." It proves a person is typing.

**Give them something they did not have.** New information is a reason to reply. "Checking in" is not.

**Ask one question they can answer in four words.** Open-ended questions are work. Yes/no or this/that gets answered from a parking lot.

## Messages that work

**Same day, after a visit:**

> Hey Dana — Dave at Affordable. Good meeting you today. The Equinox is still up front if you want to bring your husband by this week. Want me to hold it Saturday morning?

**Web lead, within twenty minutes:**

> Hi Marcus — Dave from Affordable Car Sales, got your note about the Silverado. It's here and available. Are you looking to drive it this week or still comparing?

**Price moved:**

> Morning Renee — the Tucson you looked at dropped to $9,495 today. Wanted you to hear it from me before it goes on the site. Still in the market?

**Something new arrived that fits:**

> Hey Tom — took in a 2019 Equinox LT this morning, 110k, about the same money as the one you passed on. Want photos before I list it?

**The long-shot revive, 30+ days:**

> Hi Priya — Dave at Affordable. Not selling you anything, just closing out my list. Did you end up finding something?

That last one gets a surprising number of replies, because it releases them. Plenty come back with "not yet, actually."

## Timing

- Web lead: under 20 minutes, always
- After a lot visit: same evening
- Then day 2, day 5, day 12, day 30
- After that, only when you have real news

Four touches with something to say beats fifteen "just checking in."

## Things that kill a text thread

- Paragraphs. Nobody reads a wall of text on a phone.
- Two messages in a row with no reply between them.
- Anything that reads as automated. "We value your business" is a thread ender.
- Pressure words. "Today only," "last chance," "I can't hold it much longer" when it is not true. They can tell, and the car is still on your site next week proving you wrong.

## Say who you are every time

Your number is not saved in their phone. Lead with your first name and the lot, every message, until they reply. A text from an unknown number that just says "still thinking about it?" gets deleted.

## Stop when they say stop

If someone says they bought elsewhere or asks you to stop, thank them and stop. Note it in the lead so nobody else picks it up next month. The goodwill from a clean exit is worth more than the one-in-fifty chance of wearing someone down, and the alternative is a complaint you do not need.
