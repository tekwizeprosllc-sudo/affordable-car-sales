title: Lot walk — opening lines that do not sound like a script
topic: floor
summary: The first fifteen seconds decide whether someone talks to you or waits you out. What to say instead of "can I help you find something?"
---

People arrive on a lot already braced. They have read the horror stories, they have a friend who got worked over, and they have decided in advance not to be pushed. Your opening line either confirms that fear or defuses it.

## The lines that make people shut down

"Can I help you find something?" gets "just looking" every time. It is a yes/no question, and "no" is the safe answer.

"What can I get you into today?" tells the customer you are already closing. So does "what would it take to earn your business." People recognize the vocabulary. It is the sound of being handled.

## What works instead

Give them information and an exit. No question attached.

> "Hey — I'm Dave. Keys are in the office if you want to drive anything. Take your time, I'm not going to hover."

Then walk away and mean it. Nine times out of ten they come find you inside of five minutes, and now they are approaching you. That flips the whole dynamic.

If you want to open a conversation instead, ask about the thing they are standing in front of:

> "That one just came in. You looking at trucks in general or that one specifically?"

It is a real question about their situation, and the answer tells you everything — whether they are shopping a category or have already picked.

## Ask what they are replacing

The single most useful question on a lot:

> "What are you driving now?"

It is easy to answer, nobody feels sold, and you learn their budget range, their trade, what they liked, and what finally annoyed them enough to come here. Somebody replacing a dead Cavalier and somebody replacing a lease are two different conversations.

## Read the walk

- **Straight to one car, opens the door.** They researched. Answer specifics, do not redirect them.
- **Wandering the back row.** Budget shopping. Ask what their monthly needs to look like before you walk them past anything that hurts.
- **Two people disagreeing quietly.** Sell to both. The one who is not talking usually decides.
- **Kid seat in the back of their current car.** Space and safety matter more than horsepower. Do not lead with the engine.

## Say the flaw before they find it

If the car has a scratch, point at it. If it needs tires soon, say so and say what that costs. Volunteering a flaw buys you more credibility than ten minutes of features, because now everything else you said gets believed.

Customers are not looking for a perfect car at this price point. They are looking for someone who will tell them the truth about an imperfect one.

## The part most people skip

When they leave without buying — and most will — the last line matters as much as the first:

> "No pressure at all. If it helps, I can text you if the price moves or something similar comes in. Want me to?"

Now you have permission and a reason to follow up, which is worth more than the sale you did not make today.
