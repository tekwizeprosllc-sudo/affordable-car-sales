---
title: How to sell more cars this week without discounting first
summary: Price is the last lever, not the first. Four things that move metal before you touch the number on the glass.
---

Every slow week, somebody suggests cutting prices. It is the fastest idea in the room and usually the worst one. Once you drop a number, you cannot un-drop it, and the next customer hears about it. Here is what to try before the price ever moves.

## Work the cars you already have traffic on

Pull up which vehicles got the most views and calls in the last fourteen days. Those are the cars people already want. If a unit is getting attention but not selling, the problem is almost never the price — it is the photos, a missing detail, or nobody followed up fast enough.

Fix the listing before you fix the price. Add the shots you are missing. Write two lines about why the car is worth the money. Call every person who asked about it last week.

## Answer faster than the lot down the road

Most web leads go cold because the first response takes hours. A customer who fills out a form at 7pm is sitting on the couch with their phone, looking at three other cars. If you answer at 10am the next day, you are answering someone who already moved on.

Twenty minutes is the target. Not a template — a real reply that mentions the specific car they asked about.

## Give people a reason to come in, not a reason to think

"Come take a look" is not a reason. These are:

- The car is up front, cleaned, and ready to drive right now
- We can have your trade appraised while you drive it
- We can tell you what your payment looks like before you sit down

Each one removes a thing the customer was dreading. That is what gets someone off the couch — not fifty dollars.

## Sell the inspection, not the discount

Most people buying a used car are afraid of one thing: buying somebody else's problem. If you have inspected the car, say what you checked. Brakes, tires, fluids, whatever it was. Specifics beat adjectives. "We went through it" means nothing. "New front pads, tires have about 60% left, we replaced the battery" means something.

That conversation is worth more than a price cut, because it addresses the actual objection instead of guessing at it.

## When to actually move on price

Move when the car has real age on the lot and the market has told you something — plenty of views, several calls, nobody biting. That is a signal. Move it meaningfully and reprice once, rather than shaving it three times and looking indecisive.

What does not work: quietly dropping prices across the board because the week is slow. You train your own customers to wait you out, and you give away margin on the cars that were going to sell anyway.

## The honest version

Discounting feels like doing something. Following up on twelve old leads feels like nothing. The second one makes more money almost every time, and it costs you an afternoon instead of your margin.
