---
title: Turning Ava and web leads in under 20 minutes
summary: Speed is the whole game on internet leads. Who calls, what they say, and what to do when nobody picks up.
---

A web lead is a person sitting on their couch with a phone, comparing three cars. Their interest has a half-life measured in minutes. Everything about how we handle leads should be built around that one fact.

## The twenty minute rule

Respond inside twenty minutes during open hours. Not "same day." Not "first thing tomorrow." Twenty minutes.

The customer who filled out that form at 6:40pm is still looking at cars at 7:00pm. By tomorrow morning they have either booked with somebody else or gone cold on the whole idea.

## Who owns it

One person owns internet leads per shift, and that is a named job, not something everybody does when they get a minute. Shared responsibility means nobody answers. Put the name on the whiteboard.

## What the lead already told you

Leads from the site carry context — which vehicle, what they asked, and when they want to come in. Use it. Opening with "I see you were looking at the 2019 Equinox and asked about a Saturday" proves a human read it and skips the part where they explain themselves again.

Leads from Ava carry name, phone, and usually the intent — test drive, financing, trade. Same rule.

## Call first, then text

Call once. If they do not pick up — most will not, nobody answers unknown numbers — text immediately. Do not leave a two minute voicemail, leave a ten second one:

> "Hey Marcus, Dave at Affordable Car Sales about the Silverado. I'll text you too."

Then text, because that is where the reply will come from.

## The first message

Short. Specific. One easy question.

> Hi Marcus — Dave from Affordable Car Sales. Got your note about the 2012 Silverado. It's here and ready to drive. Are you thinking this week, or still comparing?

That is it. No paragraph about our commitment to service.

## Book the appointment, not the conversation

The goal of the first contact is a time, not a relationship. Offer two specific slots rather than asking when they are free:

> "I've got tomorrow after 5 or Saturday morning. Either work?"

Two choices is easier to answer than an open calendar.

## Log it while it is fresh

Every attempt goes in the lead record with what happened. Called, no answer. Texted, asked about Saturday. Wants to see it with his wife. Two minutes of typing saves the next person from repeating your call and annoying the customer.

Move the status honestly. A lead marked "contacted" that was never actually reached is worse than one marked new, because it stops anyone else from trying.

## When they go quiet

Four touches over two weeks, then stop unless you have news. Day 1, day 2, day 5, day 12. After that, only reach out when something real changed — the price moved, a similar car came in, the one they wanted sold.

## The number that matters

Track how long it takes from lead arriving to first real human contact. Not "did we respond," but how long it took. If that number is over an hour on average, fixing it is worth more than any advertising you could buy this month.
