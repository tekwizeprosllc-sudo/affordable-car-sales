title: What "Photos Coming Soon" is costing you
topic: photos
summary: A listing with no photos gets a fraction of the views of one with twelve. Here is the actual math and the fifteen minute fix.
---

Every lot has them: three or four cars sitting on the site with a gray box that says photos coming soon. They have been there a week. Nobody is calling about them, and everybody assumes it is because the car is priced wrong.

It is not the price. Nobody has seen the car.

## The math

Shoppers filter and scroll with their eyes. A listing with no photo gets skipped in about a tenth of a second — not rejected, skipped, because there is nothing to look at. It never enters the comparison.

Think about what that means for a car sitting for an extra two weeks:

- Two weeks of floor plan or tied-up cash
- Two weeks of depreciation on a vehicle that is already aging
- A car that gets repriced down because "it isn't moving," when the real problem was that it was invisible

You end up cutting the price on a car nobody ever saw. That is the expensive part.

## Why cars end up photo-less

Usually one of these:

- It came in late and got parked in the back
- It needs a detail and nobody wants to shoot it dirty
- It has a flaw and somebody is avoiding photographing it
- Whoever takes photos was off that day

All four are process problems, not photo problems.

## The fix

**Make photos part of intake, not a separate task.** A car is not listed until it has been shot. If it is on the site, it has pictures. That rule alone solves most of it.

**Shoot it dirty rather than not at all** — but only if the alternative is waiting three days. A clean car photographs far better, so the real answer is a ten minute wash before the twelve shots. Total time from back row to live listing: about twenty-five minutes.

**Photograph the flaw.** The car with the scuffed bumper needs pictures more than the clean one, because the price only makes sense once people can see why.

## If you genuinely cannot shoot it today

Say so properly. Our listings without photos show the year, make, and model with a clear note that images are coming and an invitation to call. That is honest and it still gives the shopper something to act on.

What we never do is drop in a stock photo of a different vehicle. A customer who drives here for a car that looks nothing like the picture does not just leave — they tell people. One of those reviews costs more than a month of photo delays.

## The weekly habit

Every Monday, pull the list of live vehicles with no photos. It should be empty. If it is not, that list is the first work of the week, before follow-up, before anything.

A car with no photos is not for sale. It is just parked.
